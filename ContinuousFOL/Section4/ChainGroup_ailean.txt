Let $A$ be an MV-chain.

**Definition.** The *carry* of $a+b$ is $1$ if $a\oplus b=1$ and $0$ otherwise; the *fractional part* is $a\odot b$ in the first case and $a\oplus b$ in the second. The *digits* of $a+b+c$ are $(a\oplus b\oplus c,\ F(a,b,c),\ a\odot b\odot c)$, from which an integer part ($0$, $1$ or $2$) and a fractional part are read off.

**Lemma** (`fold_eq`). Adding $a$, $b$, $c$ from the left with carries yields the integer and fractional parts read off the digits; the digits are symmetric under all permutations of $a,b,c$.

*Proof.* Case analysis on $a\oplus b\oplus c=1$ and on $F(a,b,c)=1$, using the chain identities. Symmetry in the last two arguments is the theorem $F(x,y,z)=F(x,z,y)$; the other symmetries follow from commutativity.

**Definition** (`ChainGroup A`). Elements are pairs $(n,a)$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, $a\in A$, $a\ne1$, standing for "$n+a$". Addition is $(n,a)+(m,b)=(n+m+\mathrm{carry}(a,b),\ \mathrm{fsum}(a,b))$, zero is $(0,0)$, and the order is lexicographic.

**Theorem.** `ChainGroup A` is a linearly ordered abelian group.

*Proof.* Commutativity and the zero law are immediate; associativity follows from `fold_eq` because both bracketings compute the digits of $a+b+c$, which are symmetric. Negation of $(n,a)$ is $(-n,0)$ if $a=0$ and $(-n-1,\neg a)$ otherwise. The lexicographic order is linear because the MV-order on $A$ is, and it is translation invariant by a case analysis on carries.

**Definition** (`emb`). $u:=(1,0)>0$. Embed $A$ by $x\mapsto(0,x)$ for $x\ne1$ and $1\mapsto u$.

**Lemma.** The embedding is monotone, lands in $[0,u]$, sends only $0$ to $0$, is additive on sums without overflow ($a\odot b=0$), and satisfies $\mathrm{emb}(x\ominus y)=\max(\mathrm{emb}\,x-\mathrm{emb}\,y,0)$ and $\mathrm{emb}(\neg x)=u-\mathrm{emb}\,x$. Hence evaluating a formula in $[0,u]$ at $\mathrm{emb}\circ v$ gives the embedding of its MV-value at $v$.

*Proof.* *Monotone:* if $x\le y<1$ both images are $(0,x)\le(0,y)$ lexicographically. If $y=1$, then $\mathrm{emb}\,y=(1,0)$ exceeds every $(0,x)$, and if moreover $x=1$ the images are equal. Since $0\le x\le1$, monotonicity gives $0\le\mathrm{emb}\,x\le u$. *Only $0$ to $0$:* $\mathrm{emb}\,1=(1,0)\ne(0,0)$, and $(0,x)=(0,0)$ forces $x=0$. *Exact sums:* if $a\odot b=0$ and one of $a,b$ is $1$, the other is $0$ and the claim is trivial. Otherwise $(0,a)+(0,b)=(\mathrm{carry}(a,b),\mathrm{fsum}(a,b))$. If $a\oplus b=1$ this is $(1,a\odot b)=(1,0)=\mathrm{emb}\,1$; otherwise it is $(0,a\oplus b)$. *Truncated subtraction:* in a chain, either $x\le y$, so $x\ominus y=0$ and $\mathrm{emb}\,x-\mathrm{emb}\,y\le0$; or $y\le x$. In the second case $(x\ominus y)\odot y=x\odot\neg y\odot y=0$, so by exactness $\mathrm{emb}(x\ominus y)+\mathrm{emb}\,y=\mathrm{emb}((x\ominus y)\oplus y)=\mathrm{emb}\,x$. *Negation:* $\neg x=1\ominus x$, and $\mathrm{emb}\,x\le u$. The statement about formulae follows by induction on $\varphi$: variables agree by definition, and $\dot-$ and $\neg$ are interpreted by $\max(\cdot-\cdot,0)$ and $u-\cdot$, which match the last two identities.

**Theorem** (`exists_real_of_evalMV_chain_ne_zero`). If a Łukasiewicz formula has a non-zero value in an MV-chain, it has a non-zero value under some real $[0,1]$-assignment.

*Proof.* Its value in $[0,u]$ of the chain group is the embedding of a non-zero element, hence non-zero; now apply the transfer from ordered groups to $[0,1]$.

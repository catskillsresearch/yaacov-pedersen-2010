**Definition** (`MaximalConsistentMetric`). As before, with $\vdash_Q$ including (A10)–(A14).

**Lemma** (`consistentMetricQ_iff`). $\Gamma$ is metrically consistent iff $\Gamma\cup\mathrm{Ax}_{10\text{–}14}$ is consistent in the plain calculus, where $\mathrm{Ax}_{10\text{–}14}$ is the set of generalizations of metric axiom instances.

*Proof.* First, $\Gamma\vdash_Q\varphi$ in the metric calculus iff $\Gamma\cup\mathrm{Ax}_{10\text{–}14}\vdash_Q\varphi$ in the plain calculus. This holds by induction on derivations in each direction: a metric axiom step becomes a premise step and vice versa, and all other steps are shared. Consistency means that some formula is not provable, so it transfers through this equivalence.

**Lemmas.** Metric inconsistency iff proving $1$; a proof of $2^{-n}$ gives a proof of $1$; a metrically maximal consistent set is maximal consistent for the plain calculus (plain provability is weaker, so the archimedean clause still holds); and it is deductively closed.

*Proof.* The first three are proved exactly as in the plain calculus, using (A6) and induction on $n$. A metrically maximal consistent $\Delta$ is consistent in the plain sense because every plain proof is a metric proof. It is archimedean in the plain sense because plain proofs of $\varphi\dot-2^{-n}$ are metric proofs, and comparability is the same clause. For deductive closure: if $\Delta\vdash\varphi$, then (A1) gives $\Delta\vdash\varphi\dot-2^{-n}$ for every $n$, and the archimedean clause puts $\varphi$ in $\Delta$.

**Theorems.** Lemma 8.6, Theorem 8.10 (Zorn's lemma as before, with metric proof compactness), Lemma 8.11 and Lemma 8.12, all for the metric calculus.

*Proof.* Theorem 8.10 is reduced to the plain case. $\Gamma\cup\mathrm{Ax}_{10\text{–}14}$ is plainly consistent, so the plain Theorem 8.10 extends it to a maximal consistent $\Delta$. Because $\Delta$ contains the metric axioms, metric and plain provability from $\Delta$ coincide, and $\Delta$ is metrically maximal consistent. Lemmas 8.11 and 8.12 follow from their plain versions applied to $\Delta$, which is plainly maximal consistent by the previous lemma; equivalence is weakened to metric equivalence where needed. Lemma 8.6 repeats the plain argument with the metric adjunction lemmas. A maximal element satisfies the archimedean and comparability clauses because adjoining the relevant formula keeps consistency. Conversely, if $\Delta\subsetneq\Gamma'$ with $\varphi\in\Gamma'$ consistent, comparability with $2^{-n}$ either gives $\varphi\dot-2^{-n}\in\Delta$ for every $n$, so $\varphi\in\Delta$ by the archimedean clause, or gives $\Gamma'\vdash2^{-n}$ and hence $\Gamma'\vdash1$, which is impossible.

**Theorem** (`completeness_pre`, Theorem 9.5). If $\Gamma$ is consistent then $\Gamma$ is satisfiable (in universe $0$).

*Proof.* The Henkin model $(\mathfrak M,\sigma)$ satisfies $\Delta\supseteq e[\Gamma]$; its reduct to $\mathcal L$ gives every $\varphi\in\Gamma$ the value $\mathfrak M(e(\varphi),\sigma)=0$.

**Theorem.** Satisfiability does not depend on the universe, and $\Gamma$ is consistent iff it is satisfiable, in any universe.

*Proof.* A model in universe $0$ lifts to any universe $u$ by replacing its carrier with a copy in universe $u$; values of formulae are unchanged. Conversely, a model in any universe makes $\Gamma$ consistent by soundness (Theorem 7.2), and then the previous theorem gives a model in universe $0$. So all three conditions (consistent, satisfiable in universe $0$, satisfiable in universe $u$) are equivalent.

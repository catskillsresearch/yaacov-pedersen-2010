**Definition** (`termPreStructure`). For a set of formulae $\Delta$: the carrier is the set of terms (nonempty, it contains $v_0$); $f^{\mathfrak M}(\bar t)=f\bar t$; and $P^{\mathfrak M}(\bar t)=\sup\{p\in\mathbb D:\ p\dot-P\bar t\in\Delta\}$, which lies in $[0,1]$ (the supremum of a nonempty set of dyadics in $[0,1]$, or $0$ for the empty set).

**Lemma.** With $\sigma(x)=x$, $t^{\mathfrak M,\sigma}=t$ for every term $t$.

*Proof.* Induction on $t$. A variable $x$ is sent to $\sigma(x)=x$. For $f(t_1,\dots,t_n)$, the term pre-structure interprets $f$ as the map that builds the term $f(s_1,\dots,s_n)$ from its arguments. By the induction hypothesis the arguments evaluate to $t_1,\dots,t_n$, so the value is $f(t_1,\dots,t_n)$.

# Plan for Facts 4.1 and 4.5

The two §4 Facts that still feed the main theorems are left as `sorry` with
their official statements unchanged. This note records the paper’s wording, the
Lean, what is already proved, why one Fact does not simply reduce to the other,

## Paper wording (verbatim)

**Fact 4.1 (Weak Completeness for Łukasiewicz Logic [Cha59, RR58]).**
*Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be a Łukasiewicz propositional logic and \(\varphi \in \mathcal{S}\). Then \(\models \varphi\) if and only if \(\vdash \varphi\).*

**Fact 4.5 (Finite Strong Completeness for Continuous Logic).**
*Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be a continuous propositional logic, \(\Sigma \subseteq \mathcal{S}\) be finite, and \(\varphi \in \mathcal{S}\). Then \(\Sigma \vDash \varphi\) if and only if \(\Sigma \vdash \varphi\).*

(The paper’s §4 also records Facts 4.2–4.4. Those are not in any main-theorem
closure and remain last / optional.)

## Why Fact 4.5 does not reduce to Fact 4.1

The two languages and axiom systems are not the same:

- Łukasiewicz formulae are generated by \(\dot{-}\) and \(\neg\) (Definition 2.1),
  with axioms (A1)–(A4).
- Continuous propositional formulae also have the unary connective \(\frac12\)
  (Definition 2.4), with axioms (A5)–(A6).

A half-free conclusion can still have a continuous-logic proof that uses
\(\frac12\) and (A5)–(A6), so Łukasiewicz completeness is not a formal
corollary of continuous-logic completeness without a conservativity argument.
Conversely, \(\frac12\) is not Łukasiewicz-definable, so Fact 4.1 does not
give Fact 4.5. The useful reduction is internal to each system:

1. Prove a weak deduction theorem: \(\Sigma \cup \{\psi\} \vdash \varphi\) iff
   \(\Sigma \vdash \varphi \dot{-} n\psi\) for some \(n\).
2. Prove a semantic companion: if \(\Sigma \cup \{\psi\} \vDash \varphi\), then
   some \(n\) has \(\Sigma \vDash \varphi \dot{-} n\psi\) (compactness of
   \([0,1]^k\), or piecewise-linearity of these term functions).
3. Then finite strong completeness reduces to *weak* completeness (empty
   premises) in the same system.

Downstream, the first-order development only instantiates Fact 4.1 at two
explicit tautology families (`deductionSchema`, `swapOneIter`) and Fact 4.5
at \(\Sigma = \emptyset\). The official statements will still be proved in
full strength.

**Overall:** on the order of 4,000–7,000 lines after this checkpoint, spread
across about 6–14 further runs if each run ends at a compiling checkpoint.
Step 3 is the critical path and the main uncertainty: if Chang’s argument
goes smoothly the lower end is realistic; if representation-theoretic
lemmas balloon, the upper end is more likely.

The other three §4 Facts (4.2–4.4) are not needed for any main theorem.
They would typically follow from the same Lindenbaum / maximal-consistent
machinery once 4.1 and 4.5 are in place, and should stay last.

4. Extend the argument by \(\frac12\) and finish Fact 4.5.

## Paper wording (verbatim)

**Fact 4.1 (Weak Completeness for Łukasiewicz Logic [Cha59, RR58]).**
*Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be a Łukasiewicz propositional logic and \(\varphi \in \mathcal{S}\). Then \(\models \varphi\) if and only if \(\vdash \varphi\).*

**Fact 4.5 (Finite Strong Completeness for Continuous Logic).**
*Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be a continuous propositional logic, \(\Sigma \subseteq \mathcal{S}\) be finite, and \(\varphi \in \mathcal{S}\). Then \(\Sigma \vDash \varphi\) if and only if \(\Sigma \vdash \varphi\).*

(The paper’s §4 also records Facts 4.2–4.4. Those are not in any main-theorem
closure and remain last / optional.)

## Why Fact 4.5 does not reduce to Fact 4.1

The two languages and axiom systems are not the same:

- Łukasiewicz formulae are generated by \(\dot{-}\) and \(\neg\) (Definition 2.1),
  with axioms (A1)–(A4).
- Continuous propositional formulae also have the unary connective \(\frac12\)
  (Definition 2.4), with axioms (A5)–(A6).

A half-free conclusion can still have a continuous-logic proof that uses
\(\frac12\) and (A5)–(A6), so Łukasiewicz completeness is not a formal
corollary of continuous-logic completeness without a conservativity argument.
Conversely, \(\frac12\) is not Łukasiewicz-definable, so Fact 4.1 does not
give Fact 4.5. The useful reduction is internal to each system:

1. Prove a weak deduction theorem: \(\Sigma \cup \{\psi\} \vdash \varphi\) iff
   \(\Sigma \vdash \varphi \dot{-} n\psi\) for some \(n\).
2. Prove a semantic companion: if \(\Sigma \cup \{\psi\} \vDash \varphi\), then
   some \(n\) has \(\Sigma \vDash \varphi \dot{-} n\psi\) (compactness of
   \([0,1]^k\), or piecewise-linearity of these term functions).
3. Then finite strong completeness reduces to *weak* completeness (empty
   premises) in the same system.

Downstream, the first-order development only instantiates Fact 4.1 at two
explicit tautology families (`deductionSchema`, `swapOneIter`) and Fact 4.5
at \(\Sigma = \emptyset\). The official statements will still be proved in
full strength.

## Progress after the Lindenbaum zero-class checkpoint

The Lindenbaum quotient and the zero-class characterization are done and
`sorry`-free (`LukLindenbaum.lean`, 161 lines):

- Provable equivalence `φ ≡ ψ` iff `⊢ φ → ψ` and `⊢ ψ → φ` is an equivalence
  relation; all theorems are equivalent by weakening (A1).
- `LukLind ι` is the quotient. For any formula `ψ`, `mk φ = mk (ψ ∸ ψ)` iff
  `⊢ φ`. With `[Inhabited (LukPropForm ι)]`, this is `mk φ = 0 ↔ ⊢ φ`.
- Truncated subtraction is well-defined on the quotient, by (A2).
- (A4), written as an implication, is the *converse* contraposition
  `(¬φ → ¬ψ) → (ψ → φ)`. (Because `Imp α β = β ∸ α`, the paper’s
  `(φ ∸ ψ) ∸ (¬ψ ∸ ¬φ)` is this form, not `(φ → ψ) → (¬ψ → ¬φ)`.)
  The ex-falso seed `⊢ ¬φ → (φ → ψ)` follows immediately from (A1) and (A4).

What remains of step 3b is the Hilbert work needed to make `LukLind` an
`MVAlgebra`: double negation `φ ≡ ¬¬φ`, direct contraposition, congruence
of `¬` and of `φ ⊕ ψ := ¬(¬φ ∸ ψ)`, and then the MV identities on classes.
Double negation is the first remaining lemma.

## Route chosen for the rest of Fact 4.1

1. MV-algebra basics on the `MVAlgebra` class: natural order, lattice
   (Mundici, *Introducing MV-algebras*, Prop. 1.5), distributivity (Prop. 1.6),
   prelinearity `(x ⊖ y) ∧ (y ⊖ x) = 0` (Prop. 1.7), and
   `x ∧ y = 0 → nx ∧ ny = 0` (Lemma 1.8).
2. Linear theories: if `Γ ⊬ φ`, a theory `Γ' ⊇ Γ` maximal with `Γ' ⊬ φ` (Zorn,
   using `LukPropForm.compact`) has `LukLindT Γ'` a chain. Uses the weak
   deduction theorem (already proved) plus step 1.
3. Chain → ordered group: for an MV-chain `C`, the group `ℤ × {a ∈ C | a ≠ 1}`
   with carry `a ⊕ b = 1`, lexicographic order, and the embedding
   `C → Γ(G, (1,0))` (Mundici Ch. 2 restricted to chains; Lemma 1.2.6 (vii) of
   the tutorial gives the key cancellation identity).
4. Assembly: `Γ ⊬ φ` ⇒ `[φ] ≠ 0` in the chain `LukLindT Γ'` ⇒ `evalG ≠ 0` in its
   group ⇒ `exists_real_of_evalG_ne_zero` ⇒ `⊭ φ`. With `LukPropForm.soundness`
   and `entails_empty_iff_evalMV` this closes `luk_weak_completeness`.

Estimate for steps 1–4: about 1,200–2,000 lines. Step 3 (associativity of the
carry addition) is the largest remaining piece.

### Fidelity issue in the official Facts 4.2 and 4.3

As stated in Lean (`∀ I`), Facts 4.2 and 4.3 are false at `I = Empty`: there are
no formulas, so `∅` is satisfiable (vacuously) but not consistent
(`Consistent Σ := ∃ φ, ¬ Σ ⊢ φ`). This is proved in
`LukPropForm.consistent_iff_satisfiable_fails_empty` and
`CLPropForm.consistent_iff_satisfiable_fails_empty`. The paper tacitly has at
least one propositional variable. The official statements are left unchanged,
still `sorry`; the decision on how to restate them (for example adding
`[Nonempty I]`) is for the project owner.
